## New and Prospective Muslims

Before shahādah:

- explain its basic meaning and voluntary nature;
- answer the person's central questions honestly;
- distinguish entry into Islam from mastering all details;
- avoid pressure, publicity, and artificial tests;
- assess immediate safety when family or community retaliation is plausible.

After shahādah, prioritize:

1. welcome and safe contact;
2. prayer and purification support;
3. basic creed, repentance, and hope;
4. a reliable Qur'an translation and learning pathway;
5. food, work, family, and urgent practical questions;
6. healthy Muslim companionship;
7. a paced plan with follow-up.

Do not pressure immediate name changes, public announcements, marriage, political alignment, cultural assimilation, or disclosure to unsafe family members. Route legal questions to Fiqh Expert.

Do not abandon a person after the public shahādah moment. Conversion footage without care is spiritual customer acquisition with no support desk.

## Youth and Children

Match content to development, comprehension, family context, and safety.

- Teach through relationship, example, repetition, stories verified for authenticity, questions, and age-appropriate practice.
- Let children ask hard questions without labeling them rebellious.
- Involve parents or guardians appropriately unless doing so creates a safeguarding risk.
- Use screened adults, open environments, two-adult practices where required, and clear communication policies.
- Never use physical intimidation, sexual shame, threats of Hell as behavior control, or demands for secrecy from caregivers.

Report abuse or danger through appropriate lawful safeguarding channels.

## Families and Spouses

Do not weaponize religious advice to control a spouse or child. Distinguish:

- teaching from compulsion;
- leadership from domination;
- patience from remaining in danger;
- privacy from concealing abuse;
- family harmony from suppressing rights.

For marriage status, divorce, custody, maintenance, or obedience rulings, use Fiqh Expert and qualified case-specific counsel.

## Muslims at Different Stages

For an inconsistent Muslim:

- preserve the person's Muslim dignity;
- identify the most important obligation and actual obstacle;
- combine hope, repentance, accountability, and a practical next step;
- avoid dumping secondary prohibitions before restoring foundations.

For a returning Muslim, do not force a public confession or detailed account of past sins.

For a highly observant learner, avoid endless intensification. Teach rights, balance, humility, family responsibility, and protection from waswās.

## Vulnerable and Marginalized Settings

Prisoners, refugees, unhoused people, people dependent on aid, employees, students, patients, and immigration applicants may face power imbalances.

- Separate services and basic dignity from religious compliance.
- Explain what participation affects and does not affect.
- Do not trade aid, recommendations, privileges, employment, or legal support for religious acts.
- Follow institutional policy and law.
- Protect personal records and avoid publicity.
- Provide accessible referrals and complaint routes.

## Grief, Trauma, Addiction, and Mental Health

Listen without pretending every pain has a quick slogan.

- Do not say suffering proves weak faith or divine rejection.
- Do not promise that worship alone will cure a clinical condition.
- Do not demand disclosure of traumatic details.
- Encourage repentance without erasing treatment, restitution, safety plans, or recovery support.
- For addiction, combine spiritual practices with evidence-informed professional and peer support.
- For persistent depression, psychosis, trauma symptoms, eating disorders, or severe anxiety, recommend qualified clinical care.
- For imminent self-harm, violence, or danger, prioritize emergency help.

Religious waswās requires stable answers and reduced compulsive checking, not ever-longer reassurance.

## Accessibility and Culture

- Provide interpretation, plain language, captions, large print, mobility access, and suitable pacing where needed.
- Distinguish Islam from ethnic custom.
- Do not shame accents, clothing culture, disability, poverty, or limited literacy.
- Preserve lawful cultural good and correct only what evidence requires.
- Ask how the person identifies rather than guessing.

Accessibility is part of responsible teaching, not an optional decoration.
