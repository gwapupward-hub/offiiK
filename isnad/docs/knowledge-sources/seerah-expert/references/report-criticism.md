## 1. Why Seerah Grading Differs

The evidentiary threshold depends on the claim:

- Creed, halal and haram, legal liability, accusation, and extraordinary miracle claims require strong proof appropriate to the subject.
- Basic historical context may be reported from acceptable early material when it does not contradict stronger evidence.
- Weak reports may sometimes help identify a broad historical background when multiple indicators support it, but must not be upgraded silently.

Do not apply two opposite shortcuts: “not sahih, therefore worthless” or “found in Seerah, therefore true.”

## 2. Report Assessment Workflow

For each material report:

1. Identify the exact wording or claim.
2. Locate the earliest accessible source and all material routes.
3. Determine whether the report is musnad, mursal, mu'dal, balagh, anonymous, collective, or authorial narrative.
4. Identify transmitters, narrator dates, possible meeting, and the compiler's source.
5. Consult recognized narrator criticism where the chain is available.
6. Check whether the route depends on Ibn Ishaq's anonymous intermediary, al-Waqidi, a family isnad, or another disputed channel.
7. Compare stronger hadith, Qur'anic evidence, and verified Companion reports.
8. Compare variants for stable core and unstable detail.
9. Evaluate chronology, geography, genealogy, language, and anachronism.
10. Assign a claim-level status, not a blanket grade to the whole paragraph.

Delegate detailed narrator and hidden-defect analysis to Hadith Sciences Expert.

## 3. Corroboration

Before claiming corroboration:

- determine whether routes are genuinely independent;
- check whether later authors copied the same source;
- distinguish shared core from added detail;
- check whether weak routes share the same defect;
- and avoid strengthening a severely weak or accused route through mere repetition.

Several reports may support “an expedition occurred” without establishing its exact date, speech, participant count, or casualty figure.

Qur'anic correspondence may support an event's broad reality without authenticating every attached Seerah detail.

## 4. Text and Context Checks

Ask:

- Does the report contradict Qur'an or stronger Sunnah?
- Does it use a title, institution, coinage, place-name, or concept from a later era?
- Are travel times and locations plausible?
- Were the named people alive, present, and affiliated as claimed?
- Does a poem's language, attribution, or transmission raise concern?
- Does the report suspiciously resolve a later theological, political, or legal debate?
- Is the narrative unusually cinematic, internally omniscient, or filled with exact dialogue without a plausible witness?

Textual plausibility supports criticism but does not replace chain analysis.

## 5. Composite Narratives

When accounts differ:

- state the common core;
- list variants that affect meaning;
- prefer the stronger route with reasons;
- preserve unresolved differences;
- and avoid stitching every detail into a single uninterrupted story.

If presenting a readable synthesis, say it is a synthesis and ensure its components are compatible. Do not place reconstructed sentences in quotation marks.

## 6. Status Language

Use:

- **Authentically established:** sound hadith or equivalent strong evidence.
- **Strongly supported:** converging reliable evidence with no material conflict.
- **Acceptable historical report:** usable for history but below a sahih hadith claim.
- **Disputed:** qualified scholars or early accounts materially differ.
- **Weak:** transmission does not independently establish the claim.
- **Severely weak or rejected:** serious defect prevents meaningful reliance.
- **Unverified:** not located or insufficiently sourced.

Name the evaluated element. One event may have an established core, disputed date, weak speech, and unverified casualty figure.
