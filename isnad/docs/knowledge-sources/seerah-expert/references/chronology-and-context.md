## 1. Chronology Method

Build from secure anchors:

- the beginning of revelation;
- phases of the Makkan mission;
- migrations to Abyssinia;
- the pledges at al-'Aqabah;
- Hijrah and arrival in Madinah;
- Ramadan campaigns and named treaty periods;
- the Farewell Pilgrimage;
- the final illness and death.

Treat conventional birth and event dates as conventional when direct evidence is not decisive. “Year of the Elephant” is a relative historical anchor; do not convert it to an exact Common Era date without qualification.

Use relative order before exact dating. If sources disagree, give a range or competing sequences.

## 2. Calendars and Date Conversion

- Distinguish lunar Hijri dates from proleptic calendar calculations.
- State whether a Common Era equivalent is approximate.
- Do not assume modern tabular calendar results reproduce historical moon sighting.
- Note that Hijri year numbering begins with the Hijrah era, not necessarily with every event dated retrospectively by contemporaries.
- Distinguish a report saying “after X months” from a compiler's assigned month.

Avoid exact weekdays unless both the transmitted date and conversion method are secure.

## 3. Geographic Reconstruction

Verify:

- historical and modern place-name variants;
- direction and approximate route;
- terrain, wells, passes, caravan roads, and tribal territories;
- whether a location name refers to a broad region or exact site;
- and the uncertainty of modern identifications.

Do not draw exact routes from literary evidence that only supplies endpoints. Mark proposed locations as traditional, probable, disputed, or unknown.

## 4. Genealogy and Names

- Normalize Arabic names while preserving recognizable variants.
- Distinguish personal name, patronymic, kunyah, laqab, nisbah, clan, and tribe.
- Check maternal and paternal relationships separately.
- Do not conflate persons who share a name or kunyah.
- Identify disputed names and anonymous figures honestly.
- Treat long pre-'Adnan or ancient genealogies with appropriate caution.

Use a table when multiple people or variant names must be mapped exactly.

## 5. Arabian Context

Explain relevant context without stereotyping:

- tribal kinship, alliance, protection, blood responsibility, and clientage;
- trade routes, pastoral and agricultural economies, pilgrimage, and markets;
- Meccan sanctuary norms;
- Jewish, Christian, monotheist, and polytheist communities;
- Byzantine, Sasanian, Abyssinian, and Arabian political relationships;
- poetry, orality, writing, messengers, seals, and treaties;
- slavery, marriage practices, warfare norms, and social hierarchy.

Do not describe all pre-Islamic Arabia as culturally uniform or historically isolated. Do not map modern race, citizenship, or nation-state assumptions directly onto seventh-century identities.
