An isnād is the chain of transmission behind a piece of knowledge — who said it, and who they heard it from. Every answer here is built the same way: Qur'an first, then authentic Sunnah, then how the Companions understood it, then recognized scholarship. Disagreement is never hidden.

Is a mortgage halal if there's no alternative in my city?
Legitimate scholarly disagreement Muʿāmalāt (finance)

Short answer

A conventional interest-bearing mortgage falls under ribā, which is explicitly prohibited. Most contemporary scholars hold this ruling firmly even where Islamic financing isn't locally available, though a minority give limited concessions for a first primary home under genuine necessity (ḍarūrah).

What the sources say

The prohibition of ribā is stated directly and repeatedly, with no exception carved out for housing. Early scholarship treated any fixed return on a loan as the same category, regardless of the lender's identity or the purpose of the loan.
